How it decides what to keep

Snore Log isn't a sleep tracker that records eight hours of silence. It waits for sound and keeps only the parts worth hearing.

  1. Learns your room

    For the first few seconds it measures how loud your bedroom normally is: a fan, the heating, traffic outside.

  2. Waits for a louder sound

    A clip starts when a sound is clearly louder than the room, by +12 dB unless you change it, for at least a quarter of a second.

  3. Keeps the lead-in

    Each clip includes the 3 seconds before the sound, so you hear the whole snore, not just its peak.

  4. Stops after quiet

    After a stretch of silence you choose (2 minutes by default), the clip is saved with its date and time, and listening carries on. Clicks and rustles don't restart the countdown.

The room level keeps updating all night, using the quietest moments of the last minute. If a fan switches on at 3 am, the trigger moves up with it instead of recording until morning.

Your night, ready in the morning

Open the app with your coffee. Everything is grouped by night.

  • Play any clip

    Each recording shows when it started, how long it lasted and how loud it got. Pausing keeps your place.

  • Skim a long one

    Drag to any point, jump 10 seconds back or forward, or hold the forward button to play at 2× while you listen for the loud parts.

  • Save or share

    Send a clip to your partner, keep it in Files, or bring it to a doctor's appointment.

  • Honest gaps

    If a phone call or alarm took the microphone, a Mic off entry shows exactly which minutes weren't covered. Silence in the list really means silence.

  • Tidy up

    Delete one clip or a whole night. The list shows how much space each night uses.

Getting set up

Five minutes before your first night.

Web app

On your iPhone

  1. Open insanum.github.io/snore-log/app in Safari.
  2. Tap Share → Add to Home Screen. Open Snore Log from its icon from now on; this also keeps your recordings safe from Safari's cleanup.
  3. At bedtime: phone on charge about an arm's length away, Low Power Mode off, Do Not Disturb or Sleep Focus on.
  4. Tap Start listening, allow the microphone, then tap Dim. Leave the app open.

If Auto-Lock can't be set to Never (some work phones), that's fine: the app keeps the screen on by itself while it listens.

iPhone app

Build it yourself

  1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store and add your Apple Account in Xcode → Settings → Accounts.
  2. Clone the repository and open ios/SnoreLog.xcodeproj.
  3. In Signing & Capabilities, pick your Team. If the bundle ID is taken, change it to your own.
  4. Connect your iPhone, turn on Developer Mode, and press Run.

Private by design

A microphone app in your bedroom should earn your trust. Here's how Snore Log does.

Nothing leaves your phone

No uploads, no cloud processing. Recordings live in the app's own storage until you share or delete them.

No account, no tracking

No sign-in, ads or analytics, and no third-party code. The web app's only network request is checking this site for its own updates.

Every line is public

The code is open source, so anyone can check these claims. Read the privacy page.

Questions

Will my alarm still go off?

Yes. When iPhone needs the audio for an alarm, call or Siri, Snore Log saves the clip it was recording and steps aside. When you stop the alarm it starts listening again by itself. With the web app the iPhone is "in use" all night, so set a backup alarm on your Apple Watch itself if you rely on wrist taps.

What does the sensitivity number mean?

It's how much louder than your room a sound must be to start recording. +12 dB is a sound a bit more than twice as loud as the room's background, which catches ordinary snoring at arm's length. Raise it to +15 to +18 if a partner, fan or rustling sets it off; lower it to +8 to +10 if quiet snoring is missed. The yellow line on the live graph is the trigger level.

Why do the decibels differ from my Apple Watch?

Your watch measures absolute loudness (a quiet bedroom is around 30 to 45 dB). Snore Log measures relative to the microphone's maximum, so its numbers are negative, around −60 dB in a quiet room. It only ever compares sounds with your room's own level, so the difference doesn't matter.

What is a "Mic off" entry?

Time when Snore Log couldn't listen: an alarm, a call, Siri, or (in the web app) the screen locking or switching apps. It's listed so you know which minutes weren't covered.

Does it work on a work-managed iPhone?

The web app does, even when your employer hides Auto-Lock's Never option, because it keeps the screen on itself. The App Store app works too. Building it yourself needs Developer Mode, which some employers block.

Can it tell me if I have sleep apnea?

No. Snore Log records sound; it isn't a medical device and doesn't diagnose anything. The recordings can be useful to play for a doctor.

Is there an Android version?

The web app runs in Chrome on Android too, though it's mainly tested on iPhone. There's no native Android app.
